About Me

My background

From accounting to IT

Full Stack Developer in training

After several years of experience in accounting and administration, I made a deliberate switch to IT. My previous roles taught me to work accurately and in a structured way. I learned to keep an eye on deadlines, handle sensitive information with care and approach problems analytically.

Over the past few years, my interest in IT and technology kept growing. I became more and more curious about how websites and applications work behind the scenes. At some point I no longer wanted to just use them; I wanted to understand how they are built. That curiosity is what finally made me retrain as a developer.

What I enjoy most about programming is finding solutions. Not everything works as expected straight away, and that is exactly what makes it interesting. I try to understand where something goes wrong, test different options and adjust my approach until I find a solution. My accounting background comes in handy here, especially when it comes to logical thinking and attention to detail.

Moving into IT was a conscious choice. I know that learning to program takes time and perseverance, but I'm motivated to keep growing. By practising regularly, taking feedback on board and learning from my mistakes, I'm building my knowledge step by step.

I want to combine my previous work experience with my new technical skills to build applications that don't just work well, but are also practical and user-friendly.

My Education

Currently studying

Full Stack & AI Automation Developer

SyntraPXL

I'm taking the full-time day course Full Stack & AI Automation Developer at SyntraPXL. Step by step, this course is teaching me how to build websites and applications myself.

We started with HTML and CSS. Along the way I learned to work with Flexbox and responsive design, so websites work well on different screen sizes. Then came Bootstrap and Tailwind. With Bootstrap Grid I learned to build pages from rows and columns, while Tailwind taught me to style elements directly with classes.

The visual side of websites got plenty of attention too. I learned to use Google Fonts for typefaces, Font Awesome for icons and Coolors.co to pick colour combinations that go well together. With that knowledge I have since built several projects, including my own portfolio.

Besides building and styling websites, I also learned how relational databases work. In MySQL I practised building tables, normalising data and writing SQL queries. We're currently working on JavaScript and APIs, which is how I'm expanding my programming knowledge further.

The course also introduced me to Agile and Scrum. Among other things, we practised sprint planning, planning poker and tracking tasks on a Kanban board in Trello. That gave me a better idea of how developers work together in a team and organise their work.

My goal is to build complete web applications on my own, from the front end to the back end. I want to dive deeper into JavaScript and TypeScript, learn to work with APIs and discover how I can use AI to automate processes.

Just as important to me is that I truly understand the code I write. I don't want AI agents to generate code for me without knowing exactly what it does. I want to be able to explain how my code works, why certain choices were made and how to track down and fix bugs. I see AI as a handy tool for learning and working more efficiently, not as a replacement for my own knowledge and insight.

    Privacy policy

    Last updated on 31 October 2026

    This is my personal portfolio. I care about privacy, so I keep it simple: this site does not collect any personal data itself.

    Who is responsible?

    Wesley Bogaerts, Balen (Belgium). You can ask questions about privacy through the contact form at the bottom of the page.

    What this site does not do

    • No cookies of its own. Only the embedded Google Maps map can set cookies (see third-party services).
    • No statistics, analytics or tracking.
    • No ads and no forms that store data.

    Your theme and language choice

    If you choose the light or dark theme, or English or Dutch, your browser remembers that choice (under the names thema and taal) in the local storage of your device. That is not a cookie and it is never sent to me or to anyone else. You can clear it by deleting the site data in your browser.

    Third-party services

    To show the site, your browser loads files from these services. In doing so, they receive technical data such as your IP address and browser type:

    • GitHub Pages hosts the site (privacy statement).
    • Google Fonts provides the fonts (privacy policy).
    • Google Maps shows the map of Balen in the footer. Google may set cookies when doing so (privacy policy).
    • jsDelivr provides Bootstrap (privacy policy).
    • cdnjs (Cloudflare) provides the Font Awesome icons and the jsPDF library for the CV PDF (privacy policy).

    If you get in touch

    If you send me a message through the contact form, email me, call me or send me a message on WhatsApp or Messenger, I only use your name, contact details, message and, if provided, your company name to reply to you. I don't share them with anyone and delete them as soon as they are no longer needed. The contact form is sent through Web3Forms, which forwards your message to my inbox. WhatsApp and Messenger are Meta services with their own privacy policy.

    Links to other sites

    Links to GitHub, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Messenger and my projects open another site. The privacy policy of that site applies there.

    Your rights

    Under the GDPR you may ask which data I hold about you, and have it corrected or deleted. Just send me an email. If you are not satisfied with my answer, you can file a complaint with the Belgian Data Protection Authority.
